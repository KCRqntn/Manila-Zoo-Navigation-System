Part III (admin dashboard) counts toward **functional suitability** for groups B and C.

The characteristics follow ISO/IEC 25010:2011, which most local theses cite. The 2023 revision renamed usability to *interaction capability*, portability to *flexibility*, and added *safety*. If your adviser prefers the 2023 edition, rename those headings. The items stay the same.

Two corrections for the manuscript: the panel sheet's "ISO 2500" is **ISO/IEC 25010**, and it should be named in the objectives, e.g. *"To evaluate the system's quality in terms of functional suitability, performance efficiency, compatibility, usability, reliability, security, maintainability and portability based on ISO/IEC 25010."*

## Scoring

Compute the **weighted mean** for each item, each characteristic and overall:

WM = Σ(f × x) / N

where *f* is the number of respondents who chose a rating, *x* is the rating (1–5), and *N* is the number of respondents.

Interpret each mean with this scale:

| Weighted mean | Verbal interpretation | Quality rating |
|---|---|---|
| 4.21 – 5.00 | Strongly Agree | Excellent |
| 3.41 – 4.20 | Agree | Very good |
| 2.61 – 3.40 | Neutral | Good |
| 1.81 – 2.60 | Disagree | Fair |
| 1.00 – 1.80 | Strongly Disagree | Poor |

Report a table per characteristic (item, WM, interpretation), then a summary table of the eight characteristics and the overall mean. Add the standard deviation next to each mean if your adviser asks for it.

## Sampling suggestion

- **Group A (visitors):** at least 30, so the means are stable. Recruit on-site at the zoo or with students on a class visit.
- **Group B (zoo staff):** everyone available who will manage the system, usually 3–5.
- **Group C (IT experts):** 3–5 IT faculty or practitioners.

**Sampling technique to state in Chapter 3:** *purposive sampling* for zoo staff and IT experts (chosen for their role or expertise), and *convenience sampling* for visitors (those available at the zoo who agreed to take part). Say so plainly; the statistician will ask. Because the visitors are not a random sample, present the results as descriptive (means and SDs of this group), not as estimates for all zoo visitors.

## Instrument reliability

Before the real survey, **pilot-test** the questionnaire with about 10–15 people who are not in the final sample. Compute **Cronbach's alpha** per characteristic; 0.70 or higher is acceptable. Reword items in a characteristic that falls below 0.70, then run the real survey. Report the alpha values (pilot and final) in Chapter 3 or 4.

## Computing the results

`project-notes/survey/survey_analysis.py` produces every results table: per item and per characteristic, the N, weighted mean, standard deviation and verbal interpretation, broken down by respondent group, plus Cronbach's alpha. Type each questionnaire as one row in a copy of `survey_responses_template.csv` named `survey_responses.csv` (ratings 1–5; leave blank the items a group doesn't answer), then run `python survey_analysis.py` in that folder and open `survey_results.md`.

Report each group separately as well as combined, since visitors and IT experts judge different things.
