## Revisions from the adviser's feedback (October 2026)

These follow the adviser's design and feature feedback and should be reflected in the manuscript.

| Revision | Reason | Where addressed |
|---|---|---|
| **Visitor location.** The map can find the visitor with the phone's GPS (updated as they walk), a spot they tap on the map, or a location QR code posted at restrooms, gates, food areas and first aid. The map always states which source was used and how accurate GPS is, and warns when GPS is too rough to route from. Directions no longer assume the visitor is at the main entrance. | Directions should start from where the visitor actually is. Phone GPS is often 10–50 m off among trees and roofs, so QR codes give a verified position. | Chapter 3 (System Design, Algorithms), Scope and Limitations (GPS accuracy; HTTPS required). |
| **Nearest facility.** "Find Nearest Restroom" (also food, first aid, exit) ranks places by walking distance along the zoo's walkways and draws the route, with distance and walking time. The chatbot uses the same position and ranking. | Visitors ask for the nearest restroom more than anything else. | Chapter 3 (Algorithms: Dijkstra over walkways). |
| **QR codes.** Each animal and key facility has a printed QR sign. Scanning opens the animal (and sets the visitor's position to its exhibit) or places the visitor on the map. A defect that made every scan fail was fixed. | QR lookup is one of the thesis's core features. | Chapter 3 (System Design); Appendix (sample QR sign). |
| **Map redesign.** Category filters (animals, buildings, food, restrooms, gates) flatten other buildings; tapping a place opens a details panel; From → To directions; walkway-first "toy model" 3D style; an icon per exhibit. | Adviser feedback on usability and visual clarity. | Chapter 3 (User Interface). |
| **Game events with points.** QR scavenger hunts: visitors find animals, scan their codes, and earn points, levels and a leaderboard place. Points require a verified login. Events are split into Games and Events & News. | Adviser feedback: add games and a points system. | Chapter 3 (System Design); Scope (points need an account). |
| **Animal directory and records.** Redesigned cards (basic info on hover, full profile on click), hearts with like counts, private visitor feedback for staff, and a full admin form with photo upload and a photo gallery. Animal data was collected from the zoo's information signs (35 animals) with team photos. | Adviser feedback; the records had few animals and the admin could not edit most fields or add photos. | Chapter 3 (Data Sources). Appendix (Image Credits): some photos are cropped from zoo signs or come from online sources and must be credited. |
| **AI Guide.** A zookeeper mascot, suggested questions, new-chat button, and a bilingual (English/Filipino) spelling and grammar suggestion pop-up. Reply fixes: language detection, thanks/goodbye, diet answers, animal synonyms. | Adviser feedback and errors found in testing. | Chapter 3 (Chatbot Design). |
| **Chatbot dataset limited to Manila Zoo.** Training and test data were reviewed by meaning; questions about transport, places outside the zoo, general knowledge, animals/places not at Manila Zoo, and services with no verified information were removed (training 581 → 476, test 132 → 94). Unrelated questions now get a fallback reply instead of a trained intent. Retrained model: **91.5% accuracy, macro-F1 0.907** on the cleaned test set (previous model on the same set: 81.9%; keyword rules: 54.3%). | The chatbot should be a Manila Zoo assistant, not a city or transport guide; the earlier test set contained animals the zoo doesn't have. | Chapter 3 (Dataset), Chapter 4 (Results). Details: `ai-training/DATASET_CLEANUP.md`. |
| **Look and feel.** Pastel colour themes (five palettes, light and dark), playful stickers and background; Rules and FAQ combined into one Help page. | Adviser feedback. | Chapter 3 (User Interface). |

## Revisions made before the final defense (October 2026)

| Revision | Reason | Where addressed |
|---|---|---|
| **Visitor registration and counting.** The website QR code at the entrance opens a Visitor Registration page. A visitor creates an account on the first visit and logs in on later visits ("Register Your New Visit"). Each visit records the group: every person's age, one age category (child under 18, adult 18–59, senior citizen 60+ per RA 9994) and a PWD flag that overlaps the age categories without double counting. Visitors are counted only after the visit is saved in the database, with protection against duplicate submissions. | Gives the zoo real visitor statistics (accounts, visits, person-visits, age groups, PWDs) from the system itself. | Chapter 3 (System Design, Database Design); Chapter 4 (Results: visitor statistics). |
| **Visitor statistics in the admin dashboard.** Visitor accounts, visits and person-visits; children, adults, seniors and PWD visitors; daily and monthly charts; age distribution; visit history per visitor; CSV export (audited). | Lets staff report attendance. | Chapter 3 (Admin Module). |
| **Forgotten visitor passwords.** An administrator can give a visitor a temporary password from Visitor Registrations after checking the visitor's identity; the reset is recorded in the audit log. | Visitors at the gate can't wait for an email. | Chapter 3 (Security). |
| **AI Guide answers only from the database.** It answers the exact detail asked (habitat, diet, lifespan, status and others), refuses world trivia and rankings, names animals that aren't at Manila Zoo, and asks "which one?" when a name matches more than one animal. | Prevents invented answers. | Chapter 3 (Chatbot Design); Chapter 4 (chatbot test: 141/141 test cases passed; 3 known issues documented in `chatbot-test-report.md`). |
| **QR codes for all phones.** All codes use one style (black on white, quiet border, error correction M) with short links, tested to decode on iPhone and Android cameras; older iPhones (iOS 15.3 and older) get the 2D map automatically. | QR codes didn't open on some phones. | Chapter 3 (QR Module); Chapter 4 (compatibility). |
| **Animal records can always be deleted; nearest-place fix (November 15, 2026).** Deleting an animal that visitors had viewed or whose QR code had been scanned failed, because visit statistics pointed at it. Statistics are now kept without the link, map places showing the animal are unlinked, and the admin sees the reason if a delete is refused. "Nearest restroom/food/first aid" no longer skips a place the visitor is already standing at. | Found during the implementation audit before the final defense. | Chapter 3 (Admin Module, Database Design). |
| **Chatbot intents and dataset expanded (October 30 – November 13, 2026).** `animal_location` ("Where is the lion?") was split from `navigation` ("How do I get to the lion?"), and `out_of_scope` became a trained intent again: 10 → 12 intents. Training data grew 476 → 781 questions and the test set 94 → 138. Retrained model (November 13, 2026): **92.8% accuracy, macro-F1 0.923** (previous model on the same 138-question set: 89.1%). The keyword-rule baseline is 37.7% on this larger, harder test set (it was 54.3% on the old 94-question set), so the two baseline figures are not directly comparable. | "Where is" and "how do I get there" need different answers (the exhibit vs walking directions), and unrelated questions are recognized more reliably when the model learns them. | Chapter 3 (Dataset), Chapter 4 (Results). Details: `ai-training/data/update_dataset_2026-10-30.py`, `ai-training/model_comparison_v5.md`. |
